Tracking birthdays for an extended family — your parents,
in-laws, siblings, nieces, nephews, close friends — gets messy
fast. Here's how to set up a family birthday calendar that
actually works so no one gets forgotten.

## The three main options

### Option A: Google Calendar (most cross-platform)

**Best for:** Mixed-device families (some Android, some iPhone,
some computers).

**Setup:**

1. Open [calendar.google.com](https://calendar.google.com)
2. Click **+ Other calendars** → **Create new calendar**
3. Name it "Family Birthdays"
4. Click **Save**
5. Under **Settings** for that calendar → **Share with specific
   people** → add family member emails
6. Set their permission to "See all event details" (or "Make
   changes to events" if you want them to add too)

**Adding birthdays:**

- New event → set as **Yearly recurrence**
- Add notes (gift ideas, favorite restaurant, allergies)
- Set notifications 1 week + 1 day before

**Pros:** Works on any device with a browser. Family members
can subscribe from their phones.

**Cons:** Requires Google account. Birthdays alongside other
events in the same view can get cluttered.

### Option B: Apple Calendar with Family Sharing (easiest for all-iPhone families)

**Best for:** Households where everyone uses iPhones.

**Setup:**

1. **Settings → Family** on your iPhone → set up Family Sharing
   if not already done
2. Add family members via Apple ID
3. **Calendar app → Calendars → + Add Calendar**
4. Name it "Family Birthdays"
5. Under **People**, invite your family group

**Adding birthdays:**

- New event → set as **Repeat: Yearly**
- Alert 1 week before
- Add to "Family Birthdays" calendar

**Pros:** Tight iOS integration, syncs to everyone's iPhone
automatically. Birthdays already in Contacts show up
automatically in Calendar.

**Cons:** Excludes anyone without an iPhone. Limited to Apple
Family (5 members).

### Option C: Dedicated birthday reminder app (most focused)

**Best for:** Heavy birthday tracking with rich personalization,
especially if you have extended family with 30+ birthdays.

**Setup:**

1. Download a birthday app (we recommend
   [Birthdays](https://apps.apple.com/se/app/birthday-remind-me/id6758899294))
2. Import from Contacts (one tap, takes 30 seconds)
3. Customize reminder times per relationship type
4. Add photos to each person

**Pros:** Specifically designed for birthdays. Better reminder
options. Doesn't clutter your main calendar. Often free or
cheap.

**Cons:** Another app to maintain. Not shared automatically
across family unless app supports it.

## What to track per person

Beyond name and date, here's what saves you mental load:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Photo** | Helps when notification fires and you need to recall the person |
| **Relationship type** | Lets you set tiered reminders (mom > coworker) |
| **Birth year** | So age calculation works automatically |
| **Gift ideas** | Notes you've collected throughout the year |
| **Favorite restaurant/cuisine** | For planning meals |
| **Dietary restrictions** | Saves embarrassing gift mistakes |
| **Address** | If you might mail something |

You don't need to fill all of these for everyone. For your
inner circle, yes. For your great-uncle, just name + date is
fine.

## Reminder schedule by relationship

Different people deserve different lead times:

| Relationship | Reminder schedule |
|---|---|
| **Immediate family** (parents, partner, kids) | 1 month, 2 weeks, 1 week, 1 day, day of |
| **Extended family** (siblings, in-laws, nieces) | 1 week, 1 day, day of |
| **Close friends** | 1 week, 1 day, day of |
| **Casual friends** | 1 day, day of |
| **Coworkers** | 1 day before |

The "1 month" lead time for immediate family is important —
that's when you can still book restaurant reservations, order
custom gifts, or plan a trip.

## How to import existing birthdays fast

You probably have most family birthdays scattered across:

- Your phone's Contacts app
- Facebook (less reliable now)
- Old paper address books
- Group chats from previous years
- A spouse's memory

**Fastest approach:**

1. **Open Contacts on your iPhone**
2. **For each immediate family member: add the birthday field**
   (if not already there). Tap **Edit → Add field → birthday**.
3. **Birthday reminder apps** automatically import from
   Contacts, including the birthdays you just added.

This consolidates everything into one source of truth
(Contacts), and any future app or calendar can pull from there.

## How to keep the calendar updated

Add new people **the moment you meet them**, not later. The
"I'll remember to add them" approach fails 90% of the time.

When a new baby is born in the family — add to the calendar the
same week. When friends start dating someone serious — add the
partner's birthday after the first meeting. When you get a new
coworker — add their birthday if they share it.

Updates take 30 seconds. Trying to backfill 6 months of new
people at once takes an hour.

## When family members want to add their own

If you want others to add birthdays they know about (like your
spouse adding their family side), share editing permission:

- **Google Calendar:** Settings → Share → "Make changes and
  manage sharing"
- **Apple Calendar:** Invite as **Editor** when creating shared
  calendar
- **Birthday apps:** Look for "Shared family list" feature
  (varies by app)

The trade-off: more people editing means more chance of typos
or duplicates. Designate one "owner" who reviews additions
weekly.

## Common mistakes

❌ **Tracking only month/day without year** — you lose age
   calculation, which is half the value

❌ **Setting only day-of reminders** — too late for gifts or
   reservations

❌ **Including too many "acquaintance" birthdays** — notification
   fatigue means you start ignoring all of them

❌ **Forgetting to update when relationships change** —
   ex-partners, falling-out with friends, etc.

❌ **Relying only on Facebook** — Facebook reminders are
   unreliable, and people leave the platform

## What about Christmas, holidays, and anniversaries?

A "family birthdays" calendar can include other recurring
dates that need similar reminder logic:

- **Wedding anniversaries** — same reminder schedule as
  birthdays
- **Death anniversaries** — for grieving, you may want a 1-week
  reminder to reach out
- **Graduation dates** — for current students, useful
- **Religious holidays** — if family members celebrate
  different ones

Either add them all to "Family Birthdays" or split into a
companion "Family Dates" calendar.
