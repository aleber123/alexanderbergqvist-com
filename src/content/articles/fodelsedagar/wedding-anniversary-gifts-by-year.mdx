Anniversary gifts are loaded with tradition — and choosing
something thoughtful for each year is harder than birthdays
because there's a "correct answer" expectation. Here's the
complete year-by-year guide for both traditional and modern
gift themes, plus actual ideas that aren't clichés.

## The two main lists

**Traditional list** (origin: Europe, 1500s onward,
formalized in late 1800s): natural materials that supposedly
get stronger over time — paper, leather, wood, eventually
metals and gems.

**Modern list** (origin: 1937, US jewelers): updated for
contemporary tastes with more expensive options.

Most couples now pick whichever resonates each year. There's
no rule.

## Anniversary gift principles (regardless of year)

### What works
✅ **Handwritten card** with specific memories
✅ **An experience** to look forward to
✅ **Something that ages well** — quality over quantity
✅ **A renewal of something** — vows, a tradition, a trip
  you took early
✅ **Photos printed and framed** — not just on phones

### What flops
❌ **Generic candles** unless they specifically love a brand
❌ **Anything practical** (vacuum, kitchen utensil) — feels
   transactional
❌ **Gift cards** to anywhere generic — fine for friends,
   not anniversaries
❌ **Last year's idea repeated** in a new color

## How to plan ahead

The single best thing: mark the anniversary as a recurring
event with a 1-month reminder. That gives you time to:

- Book the restaurant or trip
- Order a custom gift
- Write a card with thought
- Coordinate with family if it's a milestone

[Birthdays](https://apps.apple.com/se/app/birthday-remind-me/id6758899294)
treats anniversaries the same as birthdays — set them up
with the right reminder schedule and never blank again.

## What to write on an anniversary card

Anniversary cards are different from birthday cards — focus
on **what's continued**, not "happy birthday."

Examples:

> "Year [X]. Still the best choice I ever made. Here's to
> the next [X] we get to spend together."

> "I love that I get to keep choosing you. Happy [X] years.
> ❤️"

> "[X] years of weird mornings, late dinners, real
> conversations and small moments I'd choose again. Happy
> anniversary."

Short is fine. Specific beats generic. Mention something
from the past year that meant something.
