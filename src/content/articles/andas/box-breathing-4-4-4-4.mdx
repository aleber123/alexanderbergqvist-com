Box breathing is probably the simplest and most reliable
breathing technique that exists. It's used by U.S. Navy SEALs
as part of their stress management, by elite athletes before
competition, and by therapists helping clients with anxiety. It
works — and you can learn it in 30 seconds.

## What is box breathing?

Box breathing is a four-step technique where each phase is
equal length:

1. **Inhale for 4 seconds**
2. **Hold for 4 seconds**
3. **Exhale for 4 seconds**
4. **Hold empty for 4 seconds**

Then you start over. You visualize a square and walk around the
sides — hence the name "box."

The technique is also called "square breathing" or "tactical
breathing" and has been used in military stress management
since the 1990s.

## Why does it work?

Box breathing activates the **parasympathetic nervous system** —
the "rest and digest" side. When parasympathetic takes over,
several things happen:

- Heart rate drops
- Blood pressure decreases
- Cortisol (stress hormone) reduces
- Brain activity shifts from amygdala (fear) to prefrontal
  cortex (logic and decision-making)

The long exhale and the pauses are key. When the exhale is as
long as the inhale — or longer — it signals to the body that
there's no threat. It's the opposite of the short, shallow
breathing that happens during stress.

The slow rhythm (4-4-4-4 = 16 seconds per cycle = ~3.75
breaths/minute) is also close to the known "coherence zone" of
5-6 breaths/minute where heart rate variability is highest.

## Step-by-step

### Sit comfortably

In a chair or on the floor. Back straight but not stiff. Hands
on your knees or thighs. Eyes can be open or closed.

### Exhale completely first

Before starting, release all the air. Clean starting point.

### Cycle 1

- **Inhale 4s:** Through the nose, fill the belly and then chest.
  Count in your head or follow a timer.
- **Hold 4s:** Stay still. No pressure — just hold.
- **Exhale 4s:** Through mouth or nose. Slow, controlled.
- **Hold empty 4s:** Lungs empty. Relax.

### Continue

Do 4-8 cycles for a quick reset. 5 minutes (~18 cycles) for
full effect on heart rate and stress hormones.

## Common mistakes

### "I forget to count"

Use a timer or an app. The Andas app has a visual box that
expands and contracts with the rhythm — no counting required.

### "I feel out of breath when I hold"

You're inhaling too deeply. Box breathing isn't deep breathing —
it's even breathing. Inhale enough to fill the lungs about
70-80%, not 100%.

### "4 seconds feels long"

Start with 3-3-3-3 for a week, then move up to 4-4-4-4. When
that feels easy, try 5-5-5-5.

### "I feel dizzy"

Unusual with box breathing, but can happen if you inhale too
deeply or tense your muscles. Relax the belly and shoulders;
inhale less deeply.

## When to use box breathing

The technique works in almost any situation where you want to
calm down. Common use cases:

| Situation | How long | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Before a meeting or presentation** | 2-3 min | Lowers heart rate, clears thinking |
| **Acute stress or anger** | 4-6 cycles | Breaks the stress reaction, gives space for thought |
| **Before sleep** | 5-10 min | Lowers activation, prepares the body |
| **Before sports competition** | 3-5 min | Calm focus without drowsiness |
| **Mid-stressful day** | 2 min | Reset without leaving your desk |
| **During panic** | Until it passes | Activates parasympathetic, breaks the spiral |

It's not ideal if you want to **wake up** or get energy — there,
Wim Hof or stimulating breathing is better. Box breathing is
sedating, not activating.

## Box breathing for different levels

**Beginner:** 4-4-4-4, 4-6 cycles per day.

**Intermediate:** 5-5-5-5, 5-10 minutes per day, often morning
and evening.

**Advanced:** 6-6-6-6 or longer. Practitioners who've trained
for months can do 8-8-8-8, producing an extremely calm state
similar to meditation.

Don't force the timings faster than feels natural. If 5-5-5-5
isn't comfortable, stay at 4-4-4-4 — that's not worse.

## What does the science say?

Box breathing isn't as well-studied as some other techniques,
but there's growing evidence that slow breathing (5-6
breaths/min) produces measurable effects:

- Lowered blood pressure (multiple studies on hypertension
  patients)
- Improved heart rate variability (HRV) — a marker of
  well-being and stress tolerance
- Reduced anxiety symptoms in short-term studies

Box breathing sits at about 3.75 breaths/minute, slightly slower
than the "coherence zone" but close enough to produce similar
effects.

Research often doesn't separate different techniques — they
measure "slow breathing" in general. But that also means box
breathing likely shares the same benefits as other slow
techniques.

## Box breathing vs other techniques

How does it differ from other popular techniques?

| Technique | Rhythm | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Box breathing** | 4-4-4-4 (even) | General stress, focus, calm |
| **4-7-8** | 4-7-8 (long exhale) | Sleep, deep relaxation |
| **Wim Hof** | Fast deep + breath holds | Energy, cold exposure |
| **Coherent breathing** | 5-5 (in-out) | HRV, daily practice |

Box breathing is the all-rounder — works in most situations and
is safe even for beginners. 4-7-8 is sharper for sleep. Wim Hof
is a completely different experience (energy rush rather than
calm).
