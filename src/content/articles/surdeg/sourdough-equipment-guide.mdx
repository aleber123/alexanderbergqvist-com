Sourdough baking can be done with $0 in additional equipment. It
can also be done with $1,000+. Here's what's worth what.

## Tier 1 — without these, you're going to struggle

### Digital kitchen scale

**Price:** $15-30

**Why:** Sourdough baking is **chemistry**. Cup measurements are too
unreliable. 100g of flour is 100g of flour, whether you pour loosely
or pack it down.

**Recommendation:** [OXO Good Grips](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=oxo+good+grips+scale&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) or
[Escali Primo](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=escali+primo+scale&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG). Both ~$25, 1g precision.

**Mistakes to avoid:** Don't buy a scale that only shows 5g
increments. And watch the battery — pick one with USB charging or
easily-replaceable batteries.

### Large mixing bowl

**Price:** $10-25

Plastic or glass. Size: 5-6 quart — you need room for dough that
doubles.

### Good oven mitts

**Price:** $20-40

Dutch ovens hit 500°F. Thin oven mitts will burn you. Get thick
ones, preferably forearm-length silicone or leather.

## Tier 2 — makes sourdough baking dramatically easier

### Banneton (proofing basket)

**Price:** $20-40 each

**Why:** Classic ring patterns on your loaf, retains moisture during
cold retard, produces evenly shaped breads.

**Recommendation:** [Rattan 9-inch round banneton](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=banneton+9+inch+round&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG)
for a 700-900g loaf. [Linen liner](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=banneton+linen+liner&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) if your
doughs often stick.

**No-cost alternative:** Kitchen bowl lined with a floured tea
towel. Works fine for your first dozen bakes.

### Lame (scoring tool)

**Price:** $10-25

**Why:** Cleaner scores on your loaf's surface. Plus control over
how the bread opens up in the oven (ear formation).

**Recommendation:** Get one with replaceable razor blades — cheaper
long-term. [UFO lame](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=UFO+lame+bread&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) or
[Wire Monkey](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wire+monkey+lame&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) are popular.

**Alternative:** A very sharp paring knife or bread knife works
OK to start.

### Dutch oven

**Price:** $40-400 depending on brand

**Why:** The single best way to create steam in a home oven. Oven
spring increases 30-50%, crust gets noticeably crustier.

**Recommendation:** 5.5 quart round, cast iron with enamel coating
(not bare cast iron — bread sticks).
[Lodge enameled cast iron](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=lodge+enameled+dutch+oven+5.5&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG)
($80) works identically to heirloom brands.
[Cuisinart 7-qt](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=cuisinart+dutch+oven+7+qt&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) often $50 on sale.
[Le Creuset 5.5qt](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=le+creuset+5.5+quart&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) is the heirloom upgrade.

**Alternative:** Two large baking sheets + a pan of boiling water
on the lower rack imitates steam less effectively.

### Bench scraper

**Price:** $5-15

**Why:** Handling dough, dividing into pieces, scraping the counter
clean after work. The single best $10 you'll spend.

**Recommendation:** [Plastic body with stainless steel edge](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=bench+scraper+stainless&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG)
— OXO, Norpro, or Ateco all work.

## Tier 3 — when you're getting serious

### Instant-read thermometer

**Price:** $20-100

**Why:** Water temperature (you want 80-85°F for mixing), dough
temperature (critical for ferment timing), bread internal temp
after baking (205-210°F means done).

**Recommendation:** [Thermopop](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=thermopop+thermometer&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) ($30) for
budget, [Thermapen ONE](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=thermapen+one&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG) ($95) if you bake constantly.
Either is a multi-decade investment.

### Pizza stone or baking steel

**Price:** $30-100

**Why:** Better heat transfer than a baking sheet → thinner bottom
crust, more crispness.

**Recommendation:** Only if you bake pizza or ciabatta regularly.
Otherwise a Dutch oven covers your loaves. Baking steel (Modernist
Cuisine, Original Baking Steel) outperforms stone but costs more.

### Pizza peel

**Price:** $20-40

**Why:** Wood or metal — slide pizza onto the stone without burning
yourself.

### Cambro container for stretch & folds

**Price:** $15-30

**Why:** Square translucent container, lets you see the bubbles
during bulk ferment from the side. Easier to track the 50-70% rise.

**Recommendation:** [Cambro 6-quart square translucent](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=cambro+6+quart+square&tag=YOUR_AMAZON_TAG).
Many pro bakers swear by these.

## Tier 4 — nerdy but not necessary

### Couche (linen proofing cloth)

**Price:** $20-40

**Why:** Classic French method for baguettes and oblong loaves.
Linen holds moisture without sticking.

### Water spray bottle

**Price:** $5-10

**Why:** Spray dough lightly with water before the oven → better
crust.

### Bread knife (scoring)

Larger than a lame, for long scores. Specialty tool, only useful
if you bake oblong loaves.

### Cooling rack

Wire grid that lets the bread cool without condensation. Most
ovens come with one — use it.

## What you DON'T need to buy

❌ **Stand mixer** — sourdough is mostly hand-mixed. Only needed
for brioche or sweet doughs.

❌ **Pasta machine** — for pasta. Not sourdough.

❌ **Vacuum sealer** — overkill for home baking.

❌ **Special "sourdough containers"** — a 1-quart Mason jar works.

❌ **"Sourdough-specific" baking sheets** — regular sheet pans are
fine.

## Realistic shopping plan

### Month 1 (~$60)
- Digital kitchen scale
- Large mixing bowl
- Good tea towel or kitchen bowl for proofing

### Month 2-3 (~$80)
- Banneton
- Lame
- Bench scraper

### Month 4+ (~$150)
- Dutch oven (Lodge or Cuisinart enamel)
- Instant-read thermometer
- Cambro container

### Later, when you're really committed
- Baking steel
- Pizza peel
- Couche
- Specialty flour subscriptions
