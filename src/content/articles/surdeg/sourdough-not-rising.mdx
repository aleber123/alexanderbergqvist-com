Nothing is more frustrating than mixing dough for 30 minutes, waiting
12 hours, and watching a flat pancake refuse to grow. Here are the
12 most common reasons your sourdough isn't rising — and exactly
what to do about each one.

## Quick diagnostic

Before going through the full list, ask yourself four questions:

1. **Is your starter active?** Does it double within 4-8 hours of feeding?
2. **Is your dough at a reasonable temperature?** 68-77°F (20-25°C)?
3. **Are you using the right amount of salt?** 2% of flour weight (10g salt per 500g flour)?
4. **Did you wait long enough?** A 70% hydration dough takes 4-6
   hours bulk ferment even with a strong starter.

If all four are yes — one of the 12 below is likely your culprit.

## The 12 most common causes

### 1. Your starter isn't active enough

**Symptoms:** starter takes more than 8 hours to double after feeding,
or doesn't double at all.

**Fix:** Feed it 2-3 times in a row every 12 hours to wake it up.
Then run the "float test" — drop a teaspoon of starter into water.
If it floats, it's ready for baking. If it sinks, feed it more.

### 2. Your kitchen is too cold

**Symptoms:** Everything is "right" but nothing is happening after 8+
hours.

**Fix:** Sourdough works 5-10× faster at 77°F (25°C) than at 64°F
(18°C). Find a warm spot — on top of the fridge, in an oven with
just the light on, near a sunny window. Buy a cheap kitchen
thermometer — many cooks discover their counter is actually 60-62°F
in winter.

### 3. Chlorinated or hard water

**Symptoms:** Starter ferments slower than online tutorials suggest.

**Fix:** Leave tap water uncovered for 30 minutes so chlorine
evaporates. Or use bottled spring water. Mineral water with natural
mineral content (Evian, Volvic) can actually boost fermentation.

### 4. Old flour

**Symptoms:** Recipes that used to work no longer produce good
fermentation.

**Fix:** Use organic flour less than 6 months old. Conventional
flour older than a year often has reduced enzyme activity. Whole
rye flour should be especially fresh — it turns rancid fastest.

### 5. Conventional flour with pesticide residues

**Symptoms:** You switched from organic to conventional and suddenly
your starter ferments poorly.

**Fix:** Some fungicides used on grain can kill wild yeast. Switch
back to organic, at least for starter feedings. Bread flour for
final doughs can usually be conventional without issue.

### 6. Too much salt

**Symptoms:** Good starter + good flour + right temperature, but
your bread still ferments poorly.

**Fix:** Weigh your salt. 2% of flour weight is the maximum. 3%+
significantly slows fermentation, 4%+ can stop it entirely. Sea
salt teaspoons vary widely from table salt — always weigh.

### 7. Too much sugar or honey

**Symptoms:** Enriched doughs (brioche, cinnamon rolls) ferment
much slower than plain bread.

**Fix:** This is normal. Sweet doughs take 1.5-2× longer to ferment.
Not a problem — just plan for it. Cold retarding 12-16 hours often
helps enriched doughs.

### 8. Drafty or uneven warmth

**Symptoms:** Part of the dough rises, part doesn't.

**Fix:** Cover with plastic wrap or a shower cap, place in a closed
container. Tutorials say "cover and let rise" for this exact reason —
even airflow matters more than people realize.

### 9. You shaped too early or too late

**Symptoms:** Bread rises but looks strange, or flattens in the
oven.

**Fix:** Shape at the right fermentation stage. Bulk ferment until
50-70% volume increase, then shape, then cold retard, then bake.
Not 100% — by then the gluten is exhausted.

### 10. Too little starter in the recipe

**Symptoms:** Everything is "right" but the bread ferments forever.

**Fix:** Many recipes use 10-20% starter relative to flour weight.
In a cold kitchen with a less-mature starter, bump it to 20-25%.
Or use a more recently fed, more active starter.

### 11. Over-developing gluten early

**Symptoms:** Stretch and fold makes the dough tight and it refuses
to rise.

**Fix:** Stretch and folds should be gentle — not like pizza dough
where you're punching in air. Soft, calm folds. Too much
manipulation tightens the gluten matrix and inhibits expansion.

### 12. The dishwasher in the same room

**Symptoms:** Yes, seriously.

**Fix:** Dishwasher vibrations during long ferments can degas your
dough during bulk fermentation. Rare but it happens. Move the dough
to the farthest corner of the kitchen during long ferments.

## When should I give up?

After 14 days of serious troubleshooting. If you've:

- Tried different flour, water, locations
- Measured temperature correctly
- Adjusted salt amounts
- Fed 4 times in a row without doubling

Then something is fundamentally wrong with the starter itself.
Easiest to start fresh — see our [Sourdough Starter From Scratch
guide](/en/surdeg/sourdough-starter-from-scratch/).

## Troubleshooting table

| Symptom | Likely cause | Quick fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing happens | Inactive starter | Feed 3 extra times |
| Slow rise | Too cold | Move to warmer spot |
| Vinegar smell | Over-mature starter | Feed more often |
| Liquid on top | Hungry starter | Feed |
| Flat loaf | Weak shaping | More stretch & folds |
| Dense crumb | Underproofed | Wait longer |
| Too many holes | Overproofed | Shorten proof time |
