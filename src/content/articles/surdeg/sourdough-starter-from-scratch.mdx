Starting your first sourdough is one of the most satisfying hobby
projects out there. It takes 7-10 days, costs almost nothing, and
ends with a living baking tradition in your kitchen cabinet that
will last a lifetime if you take care of it.

Here's exactly how to do it — step by step.

## What you'll need

- **Whole rye flour** (200-300 g / 7-10 oz) — organic if possible
- **Bread flour or all-purpose** (200-300 g) for later feedings
- **Water** — tap water works (let it sit for 30 min if it's heavily
  chlorinated)
- **Glass jar** — 0.5 to 1 liter, with a loose-fitting lid
- **Digital kitchen scale** (~$15 if you don't have one)
- **Thermometer** is nice but not critical

## Why start with whole rye?

Rye flour contains more wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria than
white wheat flour. It gets your starter fermenting noticeably
faster — usually 1-2 days quicker than starting with bread flour.
Once your starter is established (day 7+), you can gradually
transition to wheat flour if you prefer.

## Day-by-day instructions

### Day 1 — get it going

In your glass jar:

- 50 g (1.7 oz) whole rye flour
- 50 g (1.7 oz) room-temperature water (~70°F / 22°C)

Stir vigorously. You should have a thick batter, roughly the
consistency of pancake batter. Cover loosely with the lid or a
piece of paper towel.

Place at room temperature, ideally 70-77°F (21-25°C). Your kitchen
counter or on top of the fridge works well.

**What to expect:** Nothing. The batter just sits there.

### Day 2 — maybe bubbles, maybe nothing

Check the jar. You might see small bubbles on the surface or along
the sides — that's fantastic. You might see nothing — that's also
fine.

**Feed:**
- Stir everything well
- Add 25 g flour + 25 g water
- Stir until smooth

The smell may be strange (alcohol, vinegar). That's normal.

### Day 3 — the "lag phase"

Check, but don't worry if not much is happening. Many starters have
a "phase 2 dip" where it seems like everything stalled. That's the
bad bacteria dying off and the good ones taking over.

If lots of bubbles: feed like day 2.
If nothing: wait until day 4.

### Day 4-5 — it's moving

Now things should start happening. You should see:

- Bubbles throughout the batter, not just on the surface
- Volume growing between feedings
- The smell becoming "cleaner" — yogurt or apple rather than
  alcohol

**Feed every 12-24 hours:**
- Discard about half the starter (yes, throw it out — or save for
  pancakes or sourdough discard crackers)
- Add 50 g flour + 50 g water
- Stir well

### Day 6-7 — ready to bake

Signs of a mature starter:

✓ Doubles in volume 4-8 hours after feeding
✓ Bubbles throughout the mass, not just the surface
✓ Smell: mild yogurt or apple
✓ Domes up to a peak and then sinks back down

If all these signs are there — you're done. Feed one more time
with 50% rye flour + 50% bread flour to start the transition.

### Day 8 — your first bake

Use a simple beginner recipe like a classic country loaf (70%
hydration). You'll need:

- 100 g active starter (fed 4-8 hours before mixing)
- 500 g bread flour
- 350 g water
- 10 g salt

Mix, autolyse 30 min, fold every 30 min for 2-3 hours, bulk
ferment until ~50% rise (4-6 hours at room temp), shape, cold
retard overnight, bake in a Dutch oven at 500°F (260°C) for 20
min covered, then 230°C / 450°F for 25 min uncovered.

## Common problems and how to fix them

### "My starter isn't doing anything on day 5"

Possible causes:
- **Too cold:** below 65°F / 18°C the wild yeast is sluggish. Move
  it somewhere warmer (on top of the fridge, near a sunny window).
- **Chlorinated water:** switch to filtered or bottled water, or
  leave tap water uncovered for 30 min.
- **Too much wheat:** try a few feedings with pure whole rye to
  jumpstart fermentation.

### "It smells like vinegar"

Most common when the room temperature is too warm or you're not
feeding often enough. Feed more often (every 12 hours) and move
to a cooler spot.

### "There's liquid on top"

This is called "hooch" — alcohol byproduct from fermentation. It's
not dangerous, just a sign your starter is hungry. Pour off the
liquid (or stir it back in for tangier bread) and feed.

### "Mold appeared"

Mold = black, yellow, or pink fuzzy spots on the surface. Not
edible — toss everything and start over. To prevent: keep the jar
clean, use a clean spoon for each feeding, and don't seal the lid
completely tight (the starter needs to breathe).

### "It bubbles but doesn't double"

Needs more feeding cycles. Feed 2-3 times in a row every 12 hours
and test again. If still struggling, switch back to 100% rye for a
few feedings.

## Maintenance after your first bake

Once your starter is established, you have two options:

**Active at room temp:** feed every 12-24 hours. Good if you bake
2+ times a week.

**Sleep mode in the fridge:** feed once a week. Good for the
once-a-week-or-less baker. Take it out 12 hours before baking,
feed twice at room temp, then it's ready.
