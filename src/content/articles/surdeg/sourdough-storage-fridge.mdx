A sourdough starter in the fridge is the salvation for bakers who
don't bake daily. With the right routine, you feed it once a week,
forget about it the rest of the time, and still have fresh bread
whenever you want.

Here's everything you need to know.

## Dormant ≠ dead

Many beginners think the fridge "stops" the starter. That's not
accurate — everything is still working, just much more slowly. At
40°F (5°C), fermentation runs about 10× slower than at 72°F (22°C).

That means:

- **One feeding per week** replaces "every 12 hours" at room temp
- The starter collapses between feedings (normal)
- The smell becomes sharper and more sour (normal)
- "Hooch" (liquid on top) can appear after 5-7 days

## The weekly routine

For bakers who bake 1-2 times per week, here's the ideal routine:

### Sunday — feeding day
- Remove starter from fridge
- Discard 75% (compost or use for sourdough pancakes/crackers)
- Feed 1:1:1 (50g starter + 50g flour + 50g water)
- Leave at room temp 4-6 hours until doubled
- Return to fridge

### Friday — bake prep
- Remove starter
- Feed 1:2:2 or even 1:5:5 for a strong "active starter"
- 8-12 hours at room temp until peaked
- Use for baking

### Saturday — bake day
- Use peaked starter
- Bulk ferment dough, cold-retard overnight
- Bake Sunday morning

Rest of the week: the starter sleeps in the fridge.

## When you miss a week or two

It happens. Stress, travel, forgetting. Here's a reality check:

### 1-2 weeks past schedule
- Smell gets sharper
- Hooch may have accumulated (liquid on top)
- **Fix:** Pour off hooch, feed 1:1:1, back to fridge. Done.

### 2-4 weeks past schedule
- Distinct sour smell
- Possibly surface mold (black, yellow, pink) — toss if so
- **Revival:** Feed 2-3 times in a row every 12 hours at room
  temp. Each feeding discard 75%. The starter "comes back" within
  24-48 hours.

### 4-8 weeks without feeding
- Risk zone. Possibly salvageable, possibly not.
- **Attempt:** Feed 4-5 times in a row. If nothing ferments after
  two days — start over.

### More than 2 months
- Most likely start over.

## Pause for vacation

Three options for longer absences:

### Option 1: Friend feeds it
Leave instructions: "feed 1:1:1 once a week." 30 seconds of work
for them. Best option for 1-3 week trips.

### Option 2: Freeze it
- Take 50g starter
- Either spread thin on parchment to dry, or freeze as-is in a
  small container
- Store in the freezer

Thaw in the fridge 24 hours, then feed 3-4 times in a row before
use. Lasts 6-12 months frozen.

### Option 3: Dry it as a "backup"
- Spread thin on parchment paper
- Air-dry 2-3 days until completely brittle
- Crumble and store in airtight jar at room temperature
- Lasts 5+ years (verified)

To revive: mix 5g dried starter with 50g flour + 50g water. Then
feed daily 4-5 times.

This is the best long-term insurance — even pros keep a dried
backup in case their main starter gets contaminated.

## Fridge storage — best practical tips

### Keep the lid loose
Sourdough produces CO2. With a tight-sealed lid, pressure can
build up dangerously. A loose plastic lid or cloth cover works.

### Use a glass jar, not plastic
Plastic absorbs flavors and can react over time. Glass is
neutral and dishwasher-safe.

### Mark the jar with a date
"Last fed: May 4" on a piece of tape. You'll have zero memory
of when you fed it otherwise.

### Keep it small — less is more
Large starters (300+g) become sluggish. Smaller (50-100g mother)
is more active and easier to maintain. Discard generously.

## Common problems after fridge storage

**"It seems dead after a week in the fridge"**
Completely normal. Feed it 1-2 times and it wakes up.

**"It smells strong after 2 weeks"**
Hooch + fermentation byproducts. Pour off, feed 1:1:1, wait 6-8
hours, check again.

**"Help, mold"**
Mold = stopped. Toss the whole jar. Wash thoroughly with hot
soapy water before the next starter moves in.

## When to keep at room temp instead

Skip the fridge entirely if:
- You bake 3+ times per week
- You have a warm kitchen (70-77°F / 21-25°C)
- You enjoy the tang of more frequent feedings

In that case, feed twice daily (every 12 hours) and skip the
fridge step.
