Oven spring — the explosive rise in the oven — and a crispy crust
are two sides of the same coin. Here are the 7 factors and how to
control them.

## What is oven spring?

When a cold, fermented dough meets a hot oven, two things happen
simultaneously:

1. **Heat forces the gases to expand** — the dough grows rapidly
2. **The yeast works at maximum capacity** for the final 5-7
   minutes before the heat kills them

Result: a sudden "lift" of 30-50% in height. The difference between
a beautiful boule and a sad flat loaf.

## The 7 factors

### 1. Active starter

You cannot get good oven spring with a sluggish starter. Signs of
an active starter:

- Doubles within 4-8 hours after feeding
- Bubbles throughout the mass, not just the surface
- Float test: a spoonful of starter floats on water

If your starter doesn't meet these criteria — feed 2-3 extra times
before mixing.

### 2. Correct fermentation degree

**Underproofed:** the bread springs well in the oven but has a
dense crumb inside.

**Perfect:** **50-70% volume increase** during bulk ferment. Shape
at 65% increase — you still have "pep" left for oven spring.

**Overproofed:** gluten is exhausted, releases its gas, bread
flattens in the oven.

**Test:** the poke test. Press with your finger. If the indent
remains for 5+ seconds = ready for shaping. If it remains 10+
seconds or springs back immediately = over- or underproofed.

### 3. Strong shaping

Oven spring requires the dough to have **structural integrity** —
the surface should be taut like a balloon.

**Good shaping:**
- Stretch & fold 3-4 times during bulk ferment
- Confident final shaping (spread the dough, fold from outside in,
  roll into a tight boule)
- Tight boule or batard, not a slack blob

**Bad shaping:**
- Too gentle — dough is "slack" on the bench
- Disorganized folding — surface tension is gone

### 4. Oven temperature — hot, hot, hot

**Preheat:**
- Empty oven at 500°F (260°C) for 30+ minutes before baking
- With a pizza stone or Dutch oven — put it in early so it heats
  through

**During baking:**
- First 15-20 min: 475-500°F (245-260°C)
- Then lower to 425-450°F (220-230°C) to finish

Many home ovens are poorly insulated and lose 30-50°F when you
open the door. Preheat extra (20-30°F above recommended) to
compensate.

### 5. Steam — critical in the first 15 minutes

Steam does two things:
- Keeps the crust soft so the dough can expand for 5-7 min before
  the crust hardens
- Gives the final crust its glossy, crisp finish

**Best methods:**

**Dutch oven** (gold standard): the dough traps its own moisture
inside the pot. Lid on for first 20 min, off for the final 20 min.

**Sheet pan with water** (no Dutch oven): empty sheet pan on the
oven floor. When the bread goes in, pour 1 cup of boiling water
onto the pan. Close the door fast.

**Ice cube method:** drop 4-5 ice cubes onto the hot pan 1 minute
after the bread goes in. They melt slowly and release steam.

**Spray bottle:** mist the oven walls 3 times with water during
the first 2 minutes — works OK but less effective than the above.

### 6. Scoring — guides the bread's expansion

The score (cut) controls **where the bread opens up**. Without a
score, the bread cracks in a random, ugly place.

**Classic score:**
- Lame or very sharp blade
- 45° angle, fast confident motion
- Half-inch to one inch deep (1-2 cm)
- One long center cut or two in a cross

**A good score gives:**
- "Ear" — the classic raised flap where the bread opened up
- Controlled upward and outward expansion
- Decorative pattern

### 7. Final phase — let it cool properly

The crust becomes crispy **after** baking, not during. When you
take the bread out, the crust is soft. It hardens and cracks on
its own as the bread cools.

**Right:**
- Place on a wire rack (not a sheet pan — moisture gets trapped)
- Listen for the "singing" — the crust crackling as it cools
- Wait at least 1 hour before slicing

**Wrong:**
- Plastic bag immediately — soft crust guaranteed
- Slicing too early — moisture from the crumb pushes out, crust
  softens

## Common problems — bonus

### Crust darkens but bread is unevenly baked

Oven too hot in the final minutes. Lower to 425°F (220°C) earlier.

### More "ear" than Instagram-worthy

Practice. Sharper lame. Faster, more confident cut. And higher
hydration dough (75%+) often produces better ears.

### Crackling on the sides

Normal and pretty. Caused by different expansion rates between
top and bottom. Not a problem.

### Bread collapses when I take it out

Internal temperature — should be 205-210°F (96-98°C) when done.
Measure with a thermometer.

## Putting it all together

A bake that nails all 7 factors:

1. **Friday afternoon:** Feed starter (now active by evening)
2. **Friday evening:** Mix dough, autolyse 30 min, stretch & folds
   every 30 min for 2-3 hours
3. **Friday night:** Bulk ferment until 50-65% rise (4-6 h)
4. **Friday late:** Shape into tight boule, place in floured
   banneton, refrigerate
5. **Saturday morning:** Preheat oven with Dutch oven inside, 500°F
   for 30 min
6. **Bake:** Score, drop into hot Dutch oven, lid on, 20 min at
   500°F, lid off, 20 min at 450°F
7. **Cool:** Wire rack, 1-2 hours minimum before slicing
