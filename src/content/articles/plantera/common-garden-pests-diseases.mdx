Sooner or later it happens. Aphids on the roses, slugs on the
lettuce, blight on the tomatoes. Here are the most common
problems in any vegetable garden and how to fix them **without**
killing the soil ecosystem.

## Top 5 garden pests

### 1. Aphids

**Who:** small green, black, or red insects on the underside of
leaves and new growth. Common on roses, fruit trees, brassicas.

**Damage:** suck sap, cause curled leaves, secrete honeydew
that attracts sooty mold.

**Solutions ranked:**
1. **Spray off with water** — 30 sec with garden hose knocks
   50% off
2. **Soapy water** (1 tsp dish soap per cup of water) —
   repeat 3 times
3. **Ladybugs** — buy live ladybugs from garden centers
4. **Neem oil** — effective organic spray, safer for bees if
   applied in the evening
5. **Insecticidal soap** as a last resort

### 2. Slugs and snails

**Who:** lavender to dark brown gastropods, eat seedlings.

**Damage:** can wipe out a whole lettuce bed in one night.

**Solutions ranked:**
1. **Beer trap** — shallow dish at ground level, fill with cheap
   beer. They crawl in and drown
2. **Diatomaceous earth** — sharp microscopic particles cut slug
   bodies. Apply ring around plants
3. **Crushed eggshells** — same principle, cheaper
4. **Hand-picking** — at dusk with a flashlight when damp.
   Drop in soapy water
5. **Copper tape** around raised bed edges (they don't cross
   copper)
6. **Iron phosphate pellets** (organic-approved) — last resort

### 3. Cabbage worms

**Who:** velvety green caterpillars on cabbage, kale, broccoli.
Eggs from white cabbage butterflies.

**Damage:** chews large holes in leaves; can defoliate the
entire plant.

**Solutions:**
1. **Row cover** prevents eggs from landing — best prevention
2. **Hand-pick** caterpillars (they're easy to spot)
3. **Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis)** — organic bacterial spray
   targets only caterpillars, safe for everything else
4. **Companion plant marigolds and dill** nearby

### 4. Spider mites

**Who:** tiny red-brown dots on the underside of leaves,
especially in hot dry weather. Webbing is a giveaway.

**Damage:** stippled yellow spots on leaves; plant becomes weak.

**Solutions:**
1. **Spray off with water** — they hate moisture
2. **Increase humidity** — mist plants regularly
3. **Predatory mites** (sold online) — natural control
4. **Neem oil** if severe

### 5. Whiteflies

**Who:** tiny white flies that fly up when you disturb the
plant. Common on tomatoes, squash, indoor plants.

**Damage:** suck sap, secrete honeydew, vector plant viruses.

**Solutions:**
1. **Yellow sticky traps** — they fly to yellow
2. **Spray with water + soap** as for aphids
3. **Neem oil** — effective
4. **Companion plant marigolds and basil**

## Top 5 garden diseases

### 1. Tomato blight (early and late)

**Symptoms:** brown spots on leaves with yellow halos, spreads
rapidly. Late blight kills the whole plant within days.

**Causes:** humid wet weather, splash-up from soil during
watering, overcrowded plants.

**Prevention:**
- Water at the base, not on leaves
- Mulch around base to prevent splash-up
- Space tomatoes 24+ inches apart for airflow
- Stake or cage to keep leaves off the ground

**Treatment:**
- Remove infected leaves IMMEDIATELY
- Don't compost (spores survive)
- Copper spray for late blight (organic-approved)
- In severe cases, pull plants and start over with resistant
  varieties next year

**Resistant varieties:** Defiant, Iron Lady, Mountain Magic.

### 2. Powdery mildew

**Symptoms:** white powdery coating on leaves, especially
zucchini, squash, cucumbers.

**Causes:** humid weather + poor airflow.

**Treatment:**
1. **Milk spray** (1:10 ratio with water) — actually works,
   spray weekly
2. **Baking soda + water + soap** (1 tsp + 1 quart + few drops)
3. **Sulfur dust** (organic-approved fungicide)
4. **Remove worst-affected leaves** to slow spread

### 3. Blossom-end rot

**Symptoms:** black-brown patch on the BOTTOM of tomatoes,
peppers, squash.

**Cause:** calcium uptake problem from inconsistent watering
— NOT a calcium shortage in the soil.

**Fix:**
- **Consistent watering** — same amount, same days
- **Mulch** to even out soil moisture
- Don't bother adding calcium — the soil already has it; the
  issue is uneven absorption

### 4. Damping off (seedlings)

**Symptoms:** seedlings collapse at soil level shortly after
germination.

**Cause:** fungal infection, too-wet seed-starting mix.

**Prevention:**
- Use sterile seed-starting mix
- Don't overwater seedlings
- Provide good airflow with a small fan
- Sprinkle cinnamon (yes, kitchen cinnamon) on the soil — it's
  antifungal

### 5. Root rot

**Symptoms:** plants wilt despite moist soil. Roots are dark,
mushy, smelly.

**Cause:** waterlogged soil, poor drainage.

**Fix:**
- Improve drainage (raised beds, more perlite in containers)
- Reduce watering frequency
- Plants in containers: check drainage holes aren't blocked
- Severe cases: pull plant, dispose of infected soil

## Integrated approach — the 80/20 rule

In a healthy garden, 80% of pest problems handle themselves.
Beneficial insects, birds, and a balanced ecosystem do most of
the work.

Focus on:
- **Diversify plantings** — monocultures attract pests
- **Encourage beneficials** — let some plants flower (dill,
  fennel, marigolds attract beneficial wasps and ladybugs)
- **Healthy soil** — strong plants resist disease much better
- **Crop rotation** — don't grow tomatoes in the same spot 3
  years in a row

Only intervene when 20%+ of leaves/plants are affected.

## When to give up on a plant

Sometimes pulling and starting over is the right call:
- Late blight on tomatoes (spreads to neighbors)
- Verticillium wilt (in soil for years)
- Root rot beyond saving
- A plant struggling all season for unknown reasons

Pulling one plant and rotating that spot next year is wisdom,
not failure.
