Container gardening isn't "half-gardening" — it's its own
discipline with its own rules. Here's everything you need to
know to harvest lettuce in June and tomatoes in August from a
city balcony or patio.

## Quick summary

- **Check sun first** — count hours between 10am and 6pm
- **Less than 4 hours of sun** = leafy greens only
- **Large pots** beat small pots (watering becomes realistic)
- **Daily watering** in July-August
- **Raised beds** are genius for 3×3 ft balconies

## Measure your sun

Before buying a single plant — do a sun audit over a weekend:

1. **8am, 11am, 2pm, 5pm:** take a photo of your space
2. Count how many hours of direct sun actually hit the spot
3. Note **direct sun** vs. **diffuse light**

| Sun per day | What works |
|---|---|
| 0-2 hours | Shade plants, herbs (mint, chervil, parsley) |
| 2-4 hours | Leafy greens (lettuce, arugula, spinach) |
| 4-6 hours | Beans, peas, ambitious lettuce, kale |
| 6+ hours | Tomatoes, peppers, strawberries, basil |

A north-facing balcony almost always gets 0-2 hours of direct
sun. South/east/west give 4-8 hours. If you have south-facing
with no shade overhead — congratulations, you have a miniature
kitchen garden.

## What works best in containers

### Crops that succeed

**Cherry tomatoes**
- Varieties: Tumbling Tom, Sweet Million, Sungold, Tiny Tim
- Pot: 5-7 gallons (15-20 L), preferably with stake or cage
- Watering: 1-2× daily in July, once daily in June
- Harvest: August-October

**Strawberries**
- Varieties: Albion, Honeoye, Seascape; bare-root plants are
  cheapest
- Pot: Stacked, hanging baskets, or strawberry pots
- Harvest: July + a smaller batch in September

**Lettuce and arugula**
- A 3×3 ft (90×90 cm) raised bed fits 30+ plants
- Harvested all summer through successive sowing

**Herbs in pots**
- Basil, mint, parsley, dill, oregano, thyme
- 5-6 inch (12-15 cm) pots are enough
- Place by the kitchen door = more usage = more value

**Peas (climbing)**
- Large raised bed with climbing support
- Sweet sugar snaps are an incredible reward for $2 of seeds

**Marigolds + nasturtiums**
- Not food, but pollinator attractors (helps your tomatoes)
- 1-2 gallon pots, classic

### Pot size per crop

| Crop | Minimum | Optimal |
|---|---|---|
| Herbs | 5 inch / 1 quart | 6 inch / 2 quarts |
| Lettuce (1 plant) | 6 inch / 2 quarts | 8 inch / 3 quarts |
| Tomato | 12 inch / 3 gallons | 16 inch / 7 gallons |
| Strawberries | 8 inch / 1 gallon | 12 inch / 3 gallons |
| Beans | 10 inch / 2 gallons | 12 inch / 4 gallons |
| 3×3 ft raised bed | – | 4-5 cu ft of soil (3 bags) |

## Watering is everything in containers

On a balcony, pots dry out much faster than ground. On hot July
days, a 1-quart pot can **dry out in 24 hours**.

**Three rules:**
1. **Big pots** need less frequent watering (more "buffer")
2. **Morning watering** is better than evening (mildew risk)
3. **Standing water + no drainage** = root rot. Drill holes if
   the pot doesn't have any.

A great trick: **drip irrigation** with bottles you stick
upside-down into the soil. A 1-quart bottle = 3 days of
autonomy.

### Self-watering containers

If you travel or forget — invest in self-watering pots like:
- EarthBox (~$60, lasts decades)
- GroBucket DIY (5-gallon bucket conversion, ~$15)
- Sub-irrigated planters (SIP) — search YouTube

They have a water reservoir at the bottom that wicks up.
Plants can go 1-2 weeks without manual watering.

## Winter — what do you do?

Three options depending on your zone:

### Option A: Seasonal-only growing
Harvest everything in October, empty pots, store inside or in
the basement. Replant in April.

**+:** No winter work
**−:** No berries or perennials

### Option B: Winter protection (zones 5-7)
Some perennials (lavender, sage, strawberries) survive winter
in large containers if you:
- Wrap them in burlap or row cover
- Raise the pot on feet (frost from below is worst)
- Don't water frozen soil

**+:** Saves plants for next year
**−:** Pot size must be at least 7+ gallons

### Option C: Bring indoors to overwinter
Geraniums, citrus trees, rosemary: bring inside to bright
window at 40-60°F (5-15°C). Gives you greenery in winter.

**+:** Fun indoor decorations
**−:** Requires bright window and space

In zones 8+, most plants overwinter outdoors without protection.

## How to start in spring

If you're starting the 2026 season — schedule:

| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| Last week of March | Buy raised beds, potting mix, onion sets, lettuce/arugula seeds |
| 2nd week of April | Plant onion sets, direct-sow lettuce and arugula |
| 4th week of April | Indoor seedlings: tomatoes, basil on windowsill |
| Last week of May | Plant out tomatoes (after last frost) |
| June-Aug | Water, harvest, sow next round of lettuce |
| Sep-Oct | Final harvest, empty pots |

(Adjust by 2-4 weeks earlier in zones 8+, 2-4 weeks later in
zones 4-5.)
