Growing your own food is one of the few activities that's more
fun than it sounds but harder than it looks. Here's a realistic
starter guide that won't overwhelm you halfway through.

## Step 1: Start small

The most common beginner mistake is growing **too much at once**.
You buy 30 different seed packets, plant everything in April,
and by July it's mostly weeds.

Realistic first-year portfolio:

- **3-5 vegetable varieties** (max)
- **1-2 herbs**
- **A few marigolds** for pollinators

Stop there. You can expand next year.

## Step 2: Choose the right crops

For absolute beginners:

| Crop | Days to harvest | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Arugula | 30 days | Very easy |
| Lettuce | 50-60 days | Easy |
| Peas | 60-70 days | Easy |
| Radishes | 30 days | Very easy |
| Marigolds | 60 days (bloom) | Very easy |
| Dill | 50 days | Easy |
| Sunflowers | 90 days (bloom) | Easy |

Avoid your first year: tomatoes (require lots of watering +
staking), peppers (require early indoor starting), pumpkins
(take huge space).

## Step 3: Location and soil

### Location

Requirements:
- **Sun at least 6 hours/day** for vegetables (4 hours fine for
  leafy greens)
- **Wind protection** (hedge, wall, or fence)
- **Water access** (does your hose reach?)

Size:
- **2 people:** 13×13 ft (4×4 m) is plenty
- **Family of 4:** 16×20 ft (5×6 m)
- **Balcony/patio:** 2-3 raised beds (3×3 ft each)

### Soil

Buy **potting/garden mix from a nursery** — not topsoil from
Home Depot. The difference: garden mix is pH-balanced and
nutrient-rich.

For a **3×3 ft raised bed**: about 4-5 cubic feet of soil (3
bags). $30-50 for organic mix.

For **in-ground beds**: dig over your existing soil, layer 2-3
inches of garden mix on top, mix in. Add compost (chicken, cow,
or pelleted manure) as base nutrition.

### Raised beds vs. in-ground

| Raised beds | In-ground |
|---|---|
| Easier soil control | Cheaper |
| Fewer weeds | More native ecosystem |
| Warmer in spring | Better in drought |
| Better for poor native soil | Better for great native soil |
| 80% of new gardeners choose this | 20% prefer it |

For beginners with unknown native soil: raised beds.

## Step 4: Sowing and care

### Direct sowing (outdoors)

For simple crops:
1. Make furrows/holes per seed packet's depth
2. Sprinkle seeds (not too dense — read packet)
3. Press lightly
4. Water gently with a fine spray
5. Mark with a stick + label (you WILL forget what you planted)

### Indoor seedlings

More fiddly but extends your season:
1. Use seed-starting mix (not potting mix — too rich for seeds)
2. Small pots or seed trays
3. Plastic cover on top for moisture until germination
4. Light from window or LED grow light
5. Harden off — move out/in gradually for 10-14 days before
   planting out

### Watering

Critical the first week. Then it becomes routine:

- **Seedling stage:** daily light watering (soil should never
  dry completely)
- **Established plants:** 2-3 times/week, deep soak each time
- **Heat + drought:** daily, preferably morning

### Weeds

Weeds aren't the enemy — they're competition. 5-10 minutes
twice a week keeps them in check. Accept that you'll never have
a completely weed-free garden.

## Step 5: Harvest

Harvest timing isn't exact. Rules of thumb:

- **Lettuce and arugula:** cut outer leaves; the plant regrows
  2-3 times
- **Peas:** pick when pods are plump (snap) or filled
  (shelling)
- **Marigolds:** let some go to seed for next year
- **Tomatoes:** red with slight give when squeezed

## Common beginner mistakes

### 1. Underwatering and thinking you watered enough
Stick your finger in the soil — if dry 1 inch down, water
deeply.

### 2. Planting too densely
Tomatoes need 24 inches between plants. Lettuce 10 inches. You
WILL plant too densely your first year — thin bravely.

### 3. Waiting too long to harvest
Lettuce gets bitter when too large. Zucchini gets thick and
flavorless past 10 inches. Harvest early.

### 4. Forgetting to water for 5 days
It happens. Give plants a chance — water deeply and wait 24
hours before declaring them dead.

### 5. Buying too many transplants
Yes, they look beautiful at the nursery. But 30 lettuce
seedlings become a green sea in 2 weeks. Buy 8-10. Sow 8-10
more in 4 weeks.

## Your first year timeline (zones 5-7)

| Month | What to do |
|---|---|
| February | Order seeds online (better selection) |
| March | Start tomato/pepper seeds indoors |
| Early April | Sow lettuce, arugula, peas, radishes outdoors |
| Late April | Plant onion sets |
| Early May | Harden off seedlings |
| Late May | Plant out tomatoes after last frost |
| June | First harvests (radishes, arugula) |
| July-August | Peak season, daily watering, weekly harvests |
| September | Last big harvests |
| October | Clean up beds, plant garlic |
| November | Mulch beds with leaves |

Adjust by 4-6 weeks earlier in zones 8+, later in zones 3-4.

## What about next year?

Once you've survived year one (or even just half a year, that
counts), you can expand:

- **Tomatoes in containers or a greenhouse**
- **Fruit trees** (one apple, one plum)
- **Berries** (raspberries along the fence)
- **Winter lettuce and overwintering spinach**
- **Garlic** (plant in October, harvest July)
